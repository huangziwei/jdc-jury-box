## The Jury Box
by John Dickson Carr

Though the calendar may show an expiring year, once more I am compelled to write this in midsummer and in a dearth of material. Still! Since your mentor can recommend three new books, backing them up with only one triumphant classic of old, that's not a bad average for any given season.

Our very mixed bag runs thus:

**Blackstone’s Fancy**, by Richard Falkirk (Stein and Day, $6.95), roars with the second adventure of Edmund Blackstone, hardest nail of the Bow Street Runners, in the roaring eighteen twenties under roaring old King George IV. Two months ago I praised Blackstone's first recorded case, when he thwarted a plot to kidnap Princess Victoria, child heir to the throne. Here, since prizefighting is against the law, he must pretend to stamp it out while secretly training his own candidate, Ebony Joe, as an opponent for Houseman, the Bristol Butcher.

Against the world of the bare-knuckle Fancy, recreated in all its color and brutality; parade lords, scullions, ladies, harlots, bookies, hangers-on for blood-spattered pugilism which becomes also blood-spattered murder. Our protagonist needs not only fists or firearms; he needs all his wits to detect the murderer. At the end of the decade, when Peel's plans mature, surely they will make him Mr. Superintendent Blackstone, Metropolitan Police? He's too strong a character for limbo; he must not fade or disappear.

When the call-girl is found messily murdered and headless at a midtown hotel in Lillian O'Donnell's **Don’t Wear Your Wedding Ring** (Putnam, $4.95), we join the quest for her killer with Detective Third Grade Norah Mulcahaney, attractive Manhattan policewoman, and doughty Sergeant Joe Capretto. But the call-girl, it develops, was a well-to-do young married woman of good social background.

Quite a few affluent young matrons, as revealed by a second-if-less gruesome murder, have joined a part-time prostitution ring—run by whom? And why violent death as the end?
