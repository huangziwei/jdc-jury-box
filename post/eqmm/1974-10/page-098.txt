THE JURY BOX
By JOHN DICKSON CARR

Long ago, as faithful readers are aware, your mentor learned to be philosophic about books for review. Inane cheerfulness is as bad as constant gripping; let’s avoid both. If I mayn’t have items divided between detection and espionage, say two from each field for balance, let them touch as many aspects of sensationalism as possible.

And that’s what has happened this time. Beginning with sound detection against a picturesque background, we go on to grand-scale crooks’ progress from long ago, continue through sophisticated cloak-and-dagger, and end with situation mystery in which the cry of “What’s going on here?” sings like a refrain. In short, there’s a little of everything.

Throughout Bored to Death, by Michael Delving (Scribners, $6.95), the only bore we encounter is the tidal rush of water so named from the River Severn, our setting for dirty work in a corner of rural England near which I once made my home.

Exploring the Gloucestershire countryside, Dave Cannon, American book-and-antique dealer with English wife, again meets trouble. Near the river he encounters a small silver-gilt casket, finely crafted mediaeval relic; in the river he encounters the murdered body of the casket’s unsavory owner.

The theft of the silver-gilt casket—did it ever really belong to Beaver?—only complicates a problem already confused enough. Superintendent Whimbrel, suspicious of everybody, has reason for suspicion until Dave Cannon stumbles on truth. The local types are well observed, the atmosphere authentic, the crime ingenious and with clues in full view. A-plus for Michael Delving.
