## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

The month of October suggests Hallowe’en, jack-o'-lanterns, and all other japes of that night when evil spirits walk; it seems particularly fitting to report on crime.

Of four items chosen for your pleasure, the first two are fair-play detective novels, as fast-paced as they are vivid and ingenious; the third sweeps with bloody deeds of espionage-intrigue through a strife-torn Middle East; and the fourth may seem strange until examination shows that it belongs well within our province.

Thus forewarned, let’s go.

At the outset of **The Blue Hammer** by Ross Macdonald (Knopf, $7.95) we can predict that our old friend Lew Archer, Southern California’s most able private investigator, will be hired by a wealthy client to trace somebody or something mysteriously missing. But, since this author writes a true detective story, full of snares and traps, it’s the only predictable gambit.

The painting of some shapely blonde, sold as the work of a once-famous young artist, disappears from the home of millionaire Jack Biemeyer and his dissatisfied wife. Richard Chantry, the painter, himself vanished into nowhere more than two decades ago. This particular picture, if genuine and no relic of the past, proves Chantry very much alive.
