In another part of Africa, until these early 1960's called the Belgian Congo, mystery-adventure sparks **The Lingala Code**, by Warren Kiefer (Random House, $5.95). Ph.D. Michel Vernon, C.I.A. agent at the American Embassy, faces the inexplicable murder of his pal Ted Stearns, who had no enemies but was shot by some unknown hand.

Who killed Colonel Stearns, and why? We meet the beautiful Swedish girl for whom he has neglected his wife; we meet Congolese police; we meet native leaders; we uncover whole nightmares full of motives until Vernon's solution rounds out a superior thriller with overtones of cloak and dagger, too.

Still another superior thriller, also with overtones of espionage, will be found in George Beare's **The Bee Sting Deal** (Houghton Mifflin, $5.95). Used though we are to crooked escapades in high finance, this one rears its trouble-spot bridge in the East, island to Persian shore, on a scale truly gigantic. Operation Bee Sting, with wholesale massacre, can be thwarted only by wholesale adventurer Victor Stallard and Cynthia Godwin, his well-meaning if ill-mannered Girl Friday. Though pitiless Colonel Davies seems several jumps ahead, he can't stay there. At the torpedo tube of a submarine Stallard chases the bad guys' yacht and will blow her up unless she obeys. Once all echoes have faded away, everybody still alive can be happy.

And so we come round in a circle to end, as we began, with Melville Davisson Post. Since I must send you to second-hand bookshops for **Uncle Abner**, no paperback being available, let me add one other suggestion.

In addition to Abner of the mountains, this author at his best created at least four other detectives: *Monsieur Jonquelle, Prefect of Police of Paris*; Sir Henry Marquis, from *The Sleuth of St. James's Square*; Colonel Braxton, from *The Silent Witness*; Captain Walker, from *Walker of the Secret Service*. *Monsieur Jonquelle* contains one all but perfect story, "The Great Cipher."

Should you encounter any of these books in the course of your browsing, seize and hold. Or perhaps some enterprising publisher will reissue all five. We are not given many writers with the many skills of M. D. Post. Let's honor them when and while we can.
