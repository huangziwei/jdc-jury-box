Something still wrong, too. Why wouldn't the kidnappers collect their ransom money? Whatever happened to Carol during those lost days and nights, why must the girl behave so strangely now that she's safe?

But Carol is not safe; nobody is safe; as tension increases and pressures mount towards eruption. **The Captors** relies so heavily on emotion that you think it won't give all the clues; you think it can't live up to its premise or its promise. But it triumphantly succeeds in all, before a burst of gunplay and the swoop of the great falcon. Expertly done, highly recommended.

Any reader who opens John Gardner's **A Complete State of Death** (Viking, $4.95) expecting to meet Boysie Oates, that comic anti-spy of British Intelligence, instead will find a serious-minded Anglo-Italian Scotland Yard officer, Derek Torry, who has had his early police training in America. With equal skill our author can handle either wild espionage caper or the straightforward investigation by which Inspector Torry tracks and cracks a murderous crime ring of intricate structure and long arm.

One or two devotees may sigh for the zany days of Boysie Oates and his blondes; but shall a writer with so many talents as Mr. Gardner be denied the full range of versatility? And Derek Torry's Susan, before we have finished, behaves very like some free-wheeling gal from an earlier novel. If this adventure cannot be called classic detection of purest ray, it's a gripping chronicle that will hold you until the end.

**The Allingham Case-Book**, by Margery Allingham (Morrow, $5.50) contains some of the best stories ever written by my late colleague in crime. We were both taught short-story technique by one of the great editors, Reeves Shaw of the old *Strand Magazine*; and, though it seems small praise of her to say I attended the same school, she learned lessons her colleague so often bungled.

Nor, in musing over these eighteen tales, can an old friend forget the delightful weekend he spent with Margery Allingham and her husband at their Essex home more than a decade ago. Though not every story features Albert Campion, one of the best amateur sleuths, each displays his creatrix's ingenuity, charm, and acid sense of irony. All are excellent; more than a few—*Little Miss Know-All*, *One Morning They'll Hang Him*, *Joke Over*, *The Lying-in-State*—inspire profound admiration. Read 'em and see.
