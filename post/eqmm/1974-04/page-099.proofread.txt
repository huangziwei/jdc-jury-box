## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

The cards must be played as they are dealt, leaving your mentor in his far from unusual dilemma of not knowing whether to laugh or swear. Our whole theme this month is biography, from the fancied to the real.

“Fancied” biography? Well, yes. All four books are for lovers of blood and thunder. Since preliminary remarks may only confuse, let’s get down to the dirty work at once. *Arma fabulamque cano*; you’ll soon understand.

If we begin with **James Bond, the Authorized Biography of 007**, by John Pearson (Morrow, $7.95), that’s fair warning. And its publishers have been equally fair. Despite the contention that Ian Fleming’s celebrated James Bond is very much a real secret agent on the international scene, this work has been subtitled “a fictional biography”; accept as much or as little as your own credulity may direct.

It’s a fascinating chronicle. As presented by Mr. Pearson, our protagonist whirls through adventures fully as sensational as any ever dreamed by the late Mr. Fleming. All-conquering James—always James; not even his mistresses ever endanger dignity by calling him Jim—remains aloof and imperturbable whether he is cutting cards, cutting capers, or cutting throats.

The women remain cooperative, the plotters ever devious when as Bond’s substitute we foil villainy through city after city, amid luxury to cushion menace and please the Walter Mitty in us all. Except for traitors, crooked gamblers, or the misguided K.G.B., it’s top of the tree, top of the morning, top of the world.

Some biographers have seemed uncertain regarding the whereabouts of Edgar Allan Poe from January to May 1844. Plausibly disarming, **A Singular Conspiracy**, by Barry Perowne (Bobbs-Merrill, $7.95), takes the hiatus as its premise for this colorful fantasy of an unrecorded chapter in Poe’s life.
