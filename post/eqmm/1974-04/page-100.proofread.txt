All ends well, even when the real Southern planter turns up to claim that fetching English girl. Turning towards home, wife, and mother-in-law, our own master of fantasy leaves behind, as one more fact to end noble romanticizing, harassed Charles Baudelaire translating Poe's works into French. If the adventure itself never happened, Mr. Perowne's ingenuity has supplied all needs.

And now for authentic biography in paperback print.

**The Worlds of Chippy Patterson**, by Arthur H. Lewis (Pocket Books, $1.25), through journalistic eyes studies a much-admired social phenomenon—the brilliant, erratic lawyer who sways juries to his will.

For Christopher Stuart Patterson, Jr. (1875-1933) there were three worlds: that of the wealthy, aristocratic family into which he had been born; that of the criminal courts at Philadelphia's City Hall; that of his own mind and heart, much harder to define.

“Chippy” (sometimes “Chiffy”) Patterson soon defied convention. Charm and passion he brought to the defense of any who consulted him, particularly the anti-respectable; nor had he any concern for his fee. At his best in a murder trial, he scored a dazzling record of acquittals and mild sentences, seldom losing a client to the chair.

Careless, unbuttoned Chippy Patterson both worked hard and played hard until a heart attack snatched him from the arms of his latest girl-friend before any alloted span of years. There is no need to point a moral; this story rears its own monument.

Probing many ill reputations in Renaissance Italy, Maria Bellonci's **The Life and Times of Lucrezia Borgia** (Pyramid, $1.50) features the fair-haired, much-married beauty, daughter of Pope Alexander VI and sister of the murderous Cesare Borgia; who survived both as Duchess of Ferrara. She her self must be found guiltless; if she knew of crime and treachery, she had no choice but to remain silent. Here move human beings in a glittering pageant which is also sound history; with so much steel or poison, let's avoid a vicious heroine when we can.
