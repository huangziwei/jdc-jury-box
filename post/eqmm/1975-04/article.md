## The Jury Box
by John Dickson Carr

This month, while encountering no worthy new item, your mentor has had the luck to unearth four paperbacks which fulfill every requirement. Today’s paperbacks are as expensive as were hardcover novels in my youth, but with our present swollen prices the cost seems moderate by contrast. And, although classic detection is missing from the quartet, I can offer you incessant excitement either from espionage or from mystery-thriller.

**The Confidential Agent**, by Graham Greene (Pocket Books, $1.50), is a cloak-and-dagger classic first published more than thirty-five years ago, but with its grip unweakened by time.

Though the author never tells us which side in what country’s civil war is represented by a protagonist merely called D., we can soon guess. In England to buy coal from multi-millionaire Lord Benditch, hounded by peril at every step, D. soon becomes acquainted both with Lord Benditch’s handsome daughter and with the loyal little drudge called Else.

D. soon wins our sympathy as he battles danger against vivid backgrounds: the sleazy hotel in Bloomsbury, the sinister office above Oxford Street, the mining town of the Midlands, and back to a fog-shrouded London where his enemies close in.

Frame succeeds frame; he can’t seem to escape. When a last twist finds him safely homeward bound, with Rose Benditch beside him, the reader has been treated to an unforgettable experience in espionage and thrill.

Waking one morning at a Norwegian luxury hotel, Giles Denison, British protagonist of Desmond Bagley’s **The Tightrope Men** (Fawcett, $1.25), sees in his shaving mirror the face of a total stranger.

No, it’s not science fiction. By practical means, for very practical reasons, he has been given the identity of Professor Harold Meyrick, a scientific genius much older than himself. Complications fly like poisoned darts. Denison’s double life is governed by the double dealing of a hush-hush bureau from London, which must recover certain documents buried in Finland. And the real Meyrick has a daughter in whom Denison’s interest becomes anything but paternal.

Conflicting forces collide through Scandinavia with mayhem and murder. What role has been played by Kidder, the loud-voiced American? Or by Carey, that enigmatic figure from Whitehall? By the time most questions have been answered, with the wry comment that injustice rules this world, we have lived through another cloak-and-dagger epic which no connoisseur should miss.

Though **The Dark Side of the Street**, by Jack Higgins (Fawcett, 95¢), belongs to mystery-thriller rather than espionage, it has espionage atmosphere like the ticking of a bomb.

Jack Higgins, whose last novel so powerfully portrayed strife-torn Ireland, here deals with a crime ring for arranging the escape of well-heeled malefactors from prison. But will the fugitive in fact find safety? Or does his escape lead only to betrayal and death?

Paul Chavasse, from Britain’s Special Branch, must identify crime’s master plotter, the Baron, and learn the truth of this escape route by following it. Thrust behind bars as cell-mate of a youthful mug named Youngblood, Chavasse breaks jail with his cell-mate under the tutelage of the Baron. Their trail twists north to the Baron’s death-farm in Yorkshire, then oversea to the Channel Islands. Menace and violence are climaxed literally with a bang, in which the bad guys get their deserts and only Chavasse remains alive for our adventure’s spectacular end.

With Lionel White’s **The Mexico Run** (Fawcett, 95¢) we now leave British concerns for those of an American semi-hero challenging life, death, and fortune in some wickedly fast maneuvers south of the Rio Grande.

Mark Johns, not unsympathetic Vietnam veteran, won’t touch any hard drugs. But he has no objection to buying and transporting Mexican marijuana for sale in his own country.

Round his neck, as he dodges to avoid the traps of Mexico’s tricky Captain Morales, clings beautiful, blonde young Sharon, who has attached herself and won’t let go. Mark has become rather fond of Sharon before she is brutally, senselessly slain by some unknown hand. At length, fleeing from Mexico with his old friend Angel Cortillo, he also escapes legal retribution in the carefully prepared surprise finish of a very satisfactory story.
