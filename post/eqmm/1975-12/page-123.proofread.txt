From the opening pages of Clive Cussler's **Iceberg** (Dodd, Mead, $7.95), when an American Coast Guard plane sights an entire ship frozen inside an immense iceberg in the North Atlantic, it's all uncertainty or doubt. What happened to that mystery ship later? Who erased the dye marker from the ice? What's the secret undersea device of such incalculable value?

In not-so-chilly Iceland, dominated by local millionaire Oskar Rondheim and beautiful blonde millionairess Kirstie Fyrie, Major Dirk Pitt shoulders past obstacles through a not-so-secret mission in which nothing is what it seems, nothing can be taken for granted, and everything explodes into mayhem or murder.

Is the KGB our villain? Or is it somebody closer to home? Major Dirk Pitt, technically Air Force though agent for all, may seem rather too invulnerable; but, despite his buoyant emergence from trials which would kill Superman, he'll soon win your sympathy and support.

Whoever may be our principal malefactor, the reader is given fair opportunity to deduce. *Enfin*, there's one last surprise before the end of this superb intrigue, which almost demands attention from every connoisseur.

Temporarily enrolled in Britain's cloak-and-dagger brigade, Michael Mannis, news photographer of **The White Lie Assignment**, by Peter Driscoll (Lippincott, $7.50), has been told he need use no deceit except a few harmless misrepresentations when he slips into Communist-held Albania on a photographic mission.

That in itself is anything but white. Apparently Mannis has no chance as Chinese Communists battle Moscow's own in some cut-throat juggling during which perilous evidence flies from hand to hand like an atomic grenade.

Never mind these reds; a veteran British agent springs the biggest surprise. Despite all odds, after his ultimate fight in the villains' speedboat our engaging non-agent, Mannis himself, can return to his red-haired girl and triumphantly end an A-1 adventure in espionage.
