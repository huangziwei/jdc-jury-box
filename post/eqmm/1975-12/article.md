## The Jury Box
by John Dickson Carr

Friends, charge your glasses. Whether to round out the old year or usher in the new, December has proved more than fortunate. Though there are no straight detective stories, I can salute four brilliant mystery thrillers of which the first and the third, containing sound detection as well as thrill, may fairly be called memorable.

These stories alternate American with British. The protagonist of our first three books, though he is or has been a government secret agent, must deal with some private plot or crisis privately organized for sinister ends. In our fourth novel the protagonist—no secret agent, but news-magazine photographer—is the only one called on to play secret agent in a far-reaching spy design.

CIA Agent Harry Calder, posing as part of a Tokyo business firm in Robert L. Duncan's **Dragons at the Gate** (Morrow, $7.95), has no rest at all. While saving a threatened economic conference, he must learn what has happened to the disappearing Japanese gold and defeat the enemy who would ruin him with a frame.

Complicated by inter-office treachery and at least two women, from his former Japanese sweetheart to beautiful, greedy, amoral Hannah Mulridge, it's a near-deadly ordeal for Calder. But he's a good sort; he'll survive. Vivid characters, fast action, and much ingenuity of plot lend added grip to this powerful Oriental melodrama with all the evidence in sight.

The title of **The Deadly Gold**, by John Rossiter (Walker, $5.95), refers to a religious image of the Virgin, jewel-crowned, made of gold once filched by conquest, whose gory saga has not yet ended even at its mountain monastery in Spain.

Murderous Costain Quayle, British crime artist, is related to a political swell who would be embarrassed if their kinship were known. Therefore Britain's most daring secret agent, urbane Roger Tallis, must intervene when Quayle schemes to heist the golden relic from its shrine.

Drawn into the robbery by mistake, Tallis pilots a helicopter for Quayle and his savage associates, while at one side waits Carol Asher, that fetching female of status undefined. Blood flows, bullets fly, with the Guardia Civil in pursuit and death claiming one hood after another until the final shoot-out in the cave.

Can he trust Carol even then? Trust her or not, Tallis returns the golden image almost intact, for the only possible ending to a first-rate thriller which has kept every promise it made.

From the opening pages of Clive Cussler's **Iceberg** (Dodd, Mead, $7.95), when an American Coast Guard plane sights an entire ship frozen inside an immense iceberg in the North Atlantic, it's all uncertainty or doubt. What happened to that mystery ship later? Who erased the dye marker from the ice? What's the secret undersea device of such incalculable value?

In not-so-chilly Iceland, dominated by local millionaire Oskar Rondheim and beautiful blonde millionairess Kirstie Fyrie, Major Dirk Pitt shoulders past obstacles through a not-so-secret mission in which nothing is what it seems, nothing can be taken for granted, and everything explodes into mayhem or murder.

Is the KGB our villain? Or is it somebody closer to home? Major Dirk Pitt, technically Air Force though agent for all, may seem rather too invulnerable; but, despite his buoyant emergence from trials which would kill Superman, he'll soon win your sympathy and support.

Whoever may be our principal malefactor, the reader is given fair opportunity to deduce. *Enfin*, there's one last surprise before the end of this superb intrigue, which almost demands attention from every connoisseur.

Temporarily enrolled in Britain's cloak-and-dagger brigade, Michael Mannis, news photographer of **The White Lie Assignment**, by Peter Driscoll (Lippincott, $7.50), has been told he need use no deceit except a few harmless misrepresentations when he slips into Communist-held Albania on a photographic mission.

That in itself is anything but white. Apparently Mannis has no chance as Chinese Communists battle Moscow's own in some cut-throat juggling during which perilous evidence flies from hand to hand like an atomic grenade.

Never mind these reds; a veteran British agent springs the biggest surprise. Despite all odds, after his ultimate fight in the villains' speedboat our engaging non-agent, Mannis himself, can return to his red-haired girl and triumphantly end an A-1 adventure in espionage.
