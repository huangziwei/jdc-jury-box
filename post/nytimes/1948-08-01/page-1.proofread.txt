## Tales of Horror and the Supernatural

by John Dickson Carr

Machen died last December. For most of his long lifetime (he was over 80) his work had been neglected or forgotten. Now honor has been done. His **Tales of Horror and the Supernatural**, admirably edited by Philip Van Doren Stern, contains the best of his short stories, from *The Novel of the Black Seal* to a brief novel called *The Terror*. He was, as the editor somewhat diffidently points out, a Victorian romantic. Like so many of this tribe, he could do one thing supremely well: he could tell a story.

As Mr. Stern says, Machen never wrote a ghost story. His terrors well up from ancient cracks in the ground, from a demon borderland never quite defined. They are malignant; they pursue and devour. Dr. Clarke of *The Great God Pan*, keeps in a locked bureau his series of *Memoirs to Prove the Existence of the Devil*. Most of these stories are just that. Spiritually lonely, chuckling at life like the talker in the darkened room of *Hieroglyphics*, Machen had yet another quality seldom stressed. He loved the unexpected ending, the knife-twist of the sensational.

*The Cosy Room*, not included here because it is outside horror or the supernatural, might have been written by O. Henry. *The Bright Boy*, which is here in all its brilliance, might have been written by some member of the modern psychological school if one could be found able to write at all. So many of Machen’s tales are riddle stories; more than once he walks open-eyed into the detective story. If you grant one premise, then *The Terror* becomes a detective novel with all its clues fairly presented.

But over everything lies the atmosphere, the twilight of blue mists and dim yellow houses. Dyson and Phillipps prowl through their Arabian Nights London: Machen owed far more to Stevenson, another neglected artist, than the series of episodes which make up *The Three Impostors*. If Machen wrote mysteries, it can be said of his work what was said of *Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde*: that the solution is more terrifying than the mystery.
