In **The 92nd Tiger**, by Michael Gilbert (Harper & Row, $6.95), ever-reliable Mr. Gilbert turns from superior detection to a fast-paced adventure thriller vibrant with satiric comedy.

Star of British television as an all-conquering secret agent called the Tiger, for ninety-one triumphant episodes Hugo Greest has polished off the villain. Now that they're cancelling the series—despite immense popularity Hugo is almost forty—what next? There can't be any such role as the Tiger in real life?

But there can. Directed by the Foreign Office, accepting urgent invitation, our erstwhile Tiger becomes (of all things) military adviser to Sheik Ahmed of Umran in the Middle East.

And we believe it. Neither super-hero nor inept bungler, the real Hugo is intelligent, imaginative, and with a sense of humor; he captures our sympathies at once. Backed by the sheik, by the sheik's young son and by Tammy, the American heroine; Hugo coils his way through undercover dirty work to its eruption in outright war. By keeping both his head and his sense of humor, a true Briton, he emerges victorious, wins the girl, and lands an even better offer from the lords of television. Don't miss this one.
