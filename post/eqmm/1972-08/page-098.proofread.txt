## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

*Selah*! Even as your mentor climbs up to his rostrum for the reading of the lesson, permit me to announce at once that there will be corn in Egypt, good times a-coming, almost an *embarras de richesse* of the sensational. Beginning with detection extraordinary from two homicidal ladies at their slippery best, we proceed through detection in a different mood to mystery-adventure-espionage all the more deadly because every step of that narrow area has been mined or booby-trapped by the wrong enemy.

The dazzling display called **Murder-Go-Round** by Agatha Christie (Dodd, Mead, $7.95) offers *Thirteen at Dinner*, *The A.B.C. Murders*, and *Funerals Are Fatal*, three cases for Hercule Poirot spanning his creatrix's devious ways through two golden decades, '33 to '53.

*Thirteen at Dinner*, which years ago in England I read under the title of *Lord Edgware Dies*, piles illusion upon illusion when that finicky peer is stabbed under circumstances apparently damning to his light-headed wife. *The A.B.C. Murders*, by many considered Dame Agatha's masterpiece, was discussed in this column only a few months ago.

And that's not all. *Funerals Are Fatal*—through some oversight I missed it until now—will wallop you with a true Christie thunderbolt in the penultimate chapter. To this novel of rural dirty work, in my view, belongs the prize. Only a connoisseur will spot the murderer, and even he may choose the right culprit on the wrong evidence. It happened to me, I must admit; for which of us, in any fair contest, can outwit the Queen of Crime?

When first you glance at it, many elements of Ngaio Marsh's **Tied Up in Tinsel** (Little, Brown, $6.95) may seem all too familiar. At Halberds Manor, the luxurious country house of improbably named Hilary Bill-Tasman, that compulsive talker who has inherited his fortune from dubious sources, young Mr. Bill-Tasman is entertaining a Christmas party.
