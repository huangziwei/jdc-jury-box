Tad Putnam, brilliant young lawyer, proves that "Johnny" is compelled to confess: that she herself is the thief.

Though she has been bribed by an elusive con man called Heath, behind Heath lurks some unknown, shadowy figure who has masterminded it all. Aided by our repentant anti-heroine, Tad Putnam sets out to nail both Heath and the man unknown. Murder, mayhem, trickery attend the trail until a final shoot-out beside the lake traps the master plotter and brings this tale of strong action and even stronger emotion to its fitting close.

With no pause for breath James Mitchell's **Death and Bright Water** (Morrow, $6.95) sweeps us from London to Grecian isles in another series adventure whose motivation, even when murderously crooked, is still more crooked than would appear.

It seems mainly a private mission when Former Secret Agent and Professional Killer David Callan is offered riches if he will rescue Sophie Kollonaki's daughter from “protective custody” in Crete. But Callan senses the hand of the Russian K.G.B.; he has refused the offer when his old secret-service chief blackmails him to accept. And to this mission of mercy Sophie's cousin, Greek Tycoon Michael Vardakis, adds a mission of murder.

Thus for Callan, with his unfortunate little friend Lonely, begins a nightmare spy-dance of betrayal and double crossing, with no ally to be trusted and most danger where it seems safe. Surviving booby-traps as well as bullets, Callan rescues Helena Kollonaki. With the last enemy conquered, the last peril gone, Callan and Lonely look forward to a holiday which, the reader will agree, should be anywhere on earth except Crete.

**Night Judgment at Sinos**, by Jack Higgins (Fawcett, 95¢), also carries us to the Aegean, but for a different kind of thriller with its own devastating impact.

How Jack Savage, hard-bitten Irish adventurer, finds battle, intrigue, and romance in waters still seething with the ancient hatred between Greek and Turk. The aristocratic heroine, Lady Sara, proves less haughty than she looks. Narrated by as adroit a storyteller as any now in business, this is one paperback you must not miss.
