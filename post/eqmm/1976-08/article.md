## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

Your mentor, now fully recovered from a long and tedious illness, once more mounts the familiar rostrum to preach. *Plus ça change, plus ça c'est la même chose.* Let's hope this rejuvenated mentor won't bore you.

Should I try to assess books published during my absence, or ignore the hiatus and carry on?

Well, that problem has been solved by compromise. Among items dutifully read during convalescence, two were of such outstanding merit that they could not be ignored. Therefore we shall begin with those two. Both are fair-play detective novels spiced with thrill, though one depicts high-pressure modern London and the other carries us back to nineteenth-century British India at the peak of Victorian power. In conclusion, bringing us up to date, the second pair under review will be the two best novels landing on this desk in recent weeks.

**Candles for the Dead** by Harry Carmichael (Saturday Review Press, $6.95) offers another intriguing problem to the amateur detective team of Insurance Investigator John Piper and cigarette-cadging Quinn, ingenious-minded crime reporter whose first name we never hear.

David Breame, flying home to London after business in Stockholm, brings with him an elaborate cake which, he explains to the customs, he has been persuaded by a sensuous Swedish blonde to carry as a birthday present for the blonde's seven-year-old child, now in England with the child's father.

When the cake proves to have certain suspicious contents, when neither alleged child nor alleged father can be found, inexplicable violence explodes at once. David Breame's wife won't forgive his night of love with the Swedish blonde. After Breame has been tortured by two thugs for something they don't find, his innocent business partner is brutally slain in the flat Breame has shared with that unrelenting wife.

Crime Reporter Quinn takes up the trail as more murder erupts. He and Piper sift the clues, arriving at the same solution as Scotland Yard. Together they nail the principal villain of a murder-for-profit scheme far more subtle and complex than it appears. Here's sophisticated *diablerie*, highly recommended.

Rich in Victorian detail, Francis Selwyn's **Sergeant Verity and the Imperial Diamond** (Stein and Day, $7.95) glorifies Detective Sergeant William Clarence Verity, plainclothes detail, from the original Scotland Yard at Whitehall Place.

Sent to India on police duty during the savage Sepoy Mutiny of 1857, stout and pink-faced Verity swelters in formal frock coat as he attacks a mystery apparently impossible of solution. The *Kaiser-i-Hind*, a huge diamond symbolizing rule, vanishes as though by magic at the very moment it is handed over to Britain.

What Verity may lack in formal education he more than provides in native intelligence. His explanation of the vanished diamond (and who stole it) is as ingenious as it is well conceived and fair. His saga is entwined with that of Judith Perry, an English beauty who has been captured by Nana Sahib, evil genius of the mutiny.

*Enfin,* let the reader beware! Our tale has not ended even when Judith and Verity are homeward bound. There’s one last surprise, which might well have shocked Her Majesty’s government if close-mouthed Sergeant Verity had ever decided to speak.

In **Gently with the Painters** by Alan Hunter (Macmillan, $6.95) we follow Superintendent George Gently, C.I.D., through this tricky maze of murder in an English cathedral city.

Attractive young Shirley Johnson, artist-wife of a former R.A.F. hero, has been found stabbed to death behind dustbins at City Hall. Amid many would-be painters, only one of them professional but most of them eccentric, Gently stumbles through conflicting clues until the right lead sparks a disclosure few but the connoisseur will anticipate. First-rate.

If all selections this month are British, that’s how the ball bounces. And there’s some fast, expert bouncing throughout Dick Francis’s new racing epic, **High Stakes** (Harper & Row, $7.95).

Steven Scott, whose talent for inventing profitable toys earns him the wealth to buy a sound stable of horses, has been cheated of more than thirty thousand pounds by his false friend and trainer, Jody Leeds. Aided by real friend Charlie Canterfield and Alexandra Ward, the American girl, Scott contrives to settle the hash of his enemy at the climax of this really brilliant run.
