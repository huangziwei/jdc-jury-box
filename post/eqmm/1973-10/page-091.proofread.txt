In **The Tale of the Lazy Dog**, by Alan Williams (Pocket Books, 95¢), civilized but desperate amateurs have plotted the greatest air heist on record. A billion dollars in American greenbacks, flown home from the military in Southeast Asia, will be hijacked *en route* by Murray Wilde, Irish professor-adventurer, by a don't-give-a-damn South African pilot with his black navigator, and by the murderous, overfat Frenchman called Uncle Charlie.

Amid jangled nerves or treachery, with many a near-slip and with complications from Wilde's French mistress, the plan takes off amid bursting gunfire. And, incredibly, the plot succeeds; they get clean away to a safe landing. But where they land, and under what circumstances, provide the last twist as well as the last irony in a super-thriller which won't let go.

**Serpent’s Tooth**, by Sara Woods (Holt, Rinehart and Winston, $4.95), finds Antony Maitland, that engaging barrister, undertaking a defense so desperate as to seem hopeless. Young Joe Baker, who has brutally slain his kindly foster father, admits the murder but won't say why he did it. Counsel, it would seem, can do nothing. And, with the identity of the culprit already conceded, can there be any element of surprise?

Yet surprise awaits us. In the tortured Baker family we watch the unfolding of domestic drama to heights of emotional power; and, since this is fairplay detection with all the clues displayed, the startling truth constitutes another courtroom triumph for Antony Maitland and another *tour-de-force* for Sara Woods.

Like all books by Agatha Christie, Dame Agatha's **Hickory, Dickory, Death** (Pocket Books, 95¢) is conventional only in its classic pattern: background, corpse or corpses, investigation, disclosure. On the premises of a London students' hostel, with as many nationalities as conflicting passions or politics, death slips in and out at will, first by morphine poisoning and then ... well, read for yourself.

Hercule Poirot of the moustaches is on hand: *dignus*, I am happy to say, *vindice nodus*. In her long career Dame Agatha has devised countless traps or swindles for loyal fans; but at her lowest-down and most tricky, believe me, she never devised a subtler swindle than this.
