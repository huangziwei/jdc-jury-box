## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

Once more, as might have been expected, we encounter lean days. This month, it must be reported, only one new novel should merit your attraction. With reprints, however, I have had the luck of the draw. In paperback, everywhere available, you will now find three supplementary high cards: two detective classics of thirty-odd years ago, and one mystery-adventure-fantasy, with cloak-and-dagger overtones, undiminished for verve and sparkle since its first appearance in 1908.

To unravel the tangle of **And Shame the Devil**, by Sara Woods (Holt, Rinehart & Winston, $4.95), again Antony Maitland, that very sympathetic barrister-detective, champions hopeless clients as with equal skill or quixotry he juggles a hot potato.

Arkenshaw, Yorkshire, has racial (*i.e.*, prejudice?) problems not unlike our own. Two Pakistanis, newcomers to this hard-headed community, have been arrested on a charge of burglary by dour Sergeant Duckett and young Constable Ryder. Acquitted by the court, Ghulam Beas and Chakwal Mohamad bring suit for false arrest.

Briefed by the defense, Antony Maitland encounters storms domestic as well as legal. Admitting prejudice, Sergeant Duckworth, supported by his handsome daughter and fiery “Grandma,” all the same seems an honest man. But what of the evidence against him?

Though at first Maitland can see no villain, some shape of evil becomes evident when courtroom tension explodes in a poisoning mystery on the very doorstep of the law. Aided by none other than distinguished opposing counsel, Kevin O’Brien, Maitland fits together every clue. Once more Miss Woods, with all her usual ingenuity and charm of style, has written a detective novel which connoisseurs must read.
