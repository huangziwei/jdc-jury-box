## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

For all your mentor's disbelief in the element called luck, he must admit it's been a lucky season. Though we are almost halfway through the year, worthy items continue to arrive; and, despite my cynicism, I am happy to be proved wrong.

This month you are recommended to three near-masterpieces of international intrigue, each with a flavor of espionage, and the novelized account of a famous Scottish housebreaker, long unsuspected, who robbed neighbors under a most respectable mask in rowdy Edinburgh of the late eighteenth century. Specifically—

**The Left-handed Sleeper** by Ted Willis (Putnam, $7.95) opens with a jolt when Mark Ritchie, respected Member of Parliament as well as prominent personality, suddenly deserts his beautiful wife and disappears. He has bolted just ahead of British intelligence, which holds proof that Ritchie is in fact a secret Russian agent, left "sleeping" for years while he attained his trusted post.

Then begins a polite if uneasy duel between Christine Ritchie, a wife forced to acknowledge her husband's defection but still remaining faithful, and Patrick Cadwall of MI 5, who must track down the traitor but can't help sympathizing with Christine.

When Mark Ritchie is seen in Beirut, Lebanon, the pursuit at last has some clue. They must do more than nail Ritchie himself; they must identify other members of a spy ring in Britain.
