## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

With the view from my window anything but wintry, in the heat of a Southern September it's not easy to round up recommended titles for the summary in our near-Christmas issue, but I will try.

The year, predictably, has been much as usual: mingled gripes and hosannahs, with the praiseworthy in general outweighing the bad. In passing we have touched on many grave subjects, from the origin of “son of a bitch” to the history of the Black Maria Club, that projected, ill-fated society for studying real-life crimes which never quite quickened into breath. But these matters have been only in passing, as background for the parade of fiction whose rumor is now fading away.

Since I mentioned the Black Maria Club, I might add that '71 has been rich in books featuring criminals from real life. Most earnestly you are recommended to Jack McPhaul's *Johnny Torrio, First of the Gang Lords*, Paul Sann's *Kill the Dutchman!* and John Kobler's *Capone*.

Each is the biography of some once-important underworld Mr. Big, from the modest eminence of Arthur Flegenheimer ("Dutch" Schultz) to the dizzy pinnacle of Big Alphonse Capone, unmatched Grand Goblin of them all. These two met an unpleasant end: Schultz before gang bullets and Capone of creeping disease. Only Torrio, to point no moral or adorn no tale, died in the odor of sanctity after a peaceful old age.

And, while we deal with factual crimes, read *Victorian Studies in Scarlet*, by Richard D. Altick. No gangsters, it's true, but picturesque, bloodthirsty wrong 'uns from the reign of the Good Queen cut capers as well as throats through a vivid *grand guignol* of much power and charm. Yet you are most interested, perhaps, in fiction; let's turn to fiction now.
