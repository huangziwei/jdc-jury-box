The editor-in-chief, ever-reliable Ellery Queen, weighed in with *A Fine and Private Place*, domestically murderous from the first marriage proposal to the shock of the last confrontation. (By the way, he also edited three anthologies.) Emma Lathen also reliably provided a brace of adventures, *Pick Up Sticks* and *Ashes to Ashes*, for John Putnam Thatcher of the Sloan Guaranty Trust. From established writers, hardcover and paperback too, depend on Dick Francis's *Rat Race*, Sara Woods's *Let's Choose Executors*, Hugh Pentecost's *The Deadly Joke*, Hillary Waugh's *"30" Manhattan East*, Edmund Crispin's *Frequent Hearses*, Philip MacDonald's *Murder Gone Mad*. Among newcomers or comparative newcomers, salute Harry Carmichael (*Remote Control*), Ruth Rendell (*A Guilty Thing Surprised*), Catherine Aird (*A Late Phoenix*), John and Emery Bonett (*The Sound of Murder*).

Espionage? Beginning as far back as John Buchan's classic of World War I, *The 39 Steps*, we soon roar up to date with *The Poisoners* by Donald Hamilton, *Assignment—Tokyo* by Edward S. Aarons, *The Twisted Wire* by Richard Falkirk, *Place of Mists* by Robert MacLeod. Ranging over all the earth for its betrayals, here is cloak-and-dagger slipperiness of the true stamp or vintage; don't miss any title.

This year I have even ventured to review criticism, with deserved praise for *The Mystery Writer's Art* by Francis M. Nevins and *A Catalogue of Crime* by Jacques Barzun and Wendell Hertig Taylor. But such remarks stray from the point, to which we must return.

Rounding out our three categories of sensationalism with mystery-adventure, then, let me give you four mystery-adventures as strong as the detective stories or the espionage. From Victor Canning's *The Great Affair* and Jack Higgins's *Night Adventure at Sinos* to Robert L. Fish's *The Green Hell Treasure* and Whit Masterson's *The Gravy Train* they are loaded with high explosives. The two last-named, presenting *every* clue, might well be called detection; and, though a critic dare pronounce nothing best, I enjoyed *The Gravy Train* more than any novel I have read this year.

Is it a good omen for the future? *Deo volente*, let's hope so!
