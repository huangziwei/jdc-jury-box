## The Jury Box

by John Dickson Carr

And now, past the year’s middle, we encounter three thrillers—the first of them a near-detective novel—which rank as sound and praiseworthy rather than distinguished, although they are backed up by one really notable achievement, a vast and erudite compendium of information about your favorite reading matter. If I can’t cheer for any except the last-named, with entire justice I can commend. There’s no occasion to gripe.

**Legend in Green Velvet**, by Elizabeth Peters (Dodd, Mead, $7.95), carries us to Scotland with our own attractive Susan, romantic-minded American girl who has been hired to assist a famous Scots archaeologist in his quest after ancient secrets.

Even before leaving Edinburgh for Professor Campbell’s dig, Susan is swept into a whirl of sensationalism, stumbles across a murder, and among other enigmatic characters meets a young laird of more than picturesque heritage.

Vivid background as well as authentic history animate rapid-paced scenes which almost become detection. Though the author might have given us more clues to her master criminal, on page 221 she explodes a genuine surprise. Thenceforward, despite so many stabs of danger, the saga of Susan and her young laird whirls on to its satisfactorily happy end.
